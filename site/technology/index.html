THE TECHNOLOGY BEHIND YOUR RUN

Built on Apple’s
workout technology.

Runner records your run with Apple’s workout APIs and uses the watch’s measurements for heart rate, distance, and outdoor running speed. Core Location supplies GPS positions. Runner brings those measurements into view and adds calculations for metrics such as average pace and lap pace.

Your workout and available route are saved to Apple Health, with your permission.

THE SOURCE OF EACH NUMBER

Where your metrics come from

Apple provides the core measurements.
Here is what Runner does with them.

Data sources and Runner’s calculations for each running metric
MetricSource and calculation
Outdoor pace PACEApple’s running speed from HealthKit, converted to minutes per kilometre or mile.
Distance KM / MIHealthKit is the primary source. Outdoors, Runner uses filtered GPS distance as a fallback until HealthKit provides distance.
Heart rate & active time BPM / TIMEHeart rate comes from HealthKit. Active time comes from Apple’s workout builder and excludes pauses.
Average pace AVG PACERunner calculates pace from distance and active time, smoothing the live display. The final summary uses the total distance and active time without that smoothing.
Lap pace LAP PACERunner calculates pace for automatic distance laps, using distance and active time and estimating when each lap boundary was crossed.
Elevation gain ALT GAINRunner calculates climbs from filtered altitude readings supplied by Core Location. Available outdoors only.
Indoor pace PACEHealthKit supplies motion-based distance. Runner calculates current pace from changes in that distance over a short window of active time. No GPS is used.
Heart rate zones ZONESZone boundaries and time in each zone come from HealthKit on watchOS 27 or later. Runner uses your current heart rate to show your position within those zones.

WHAT RUNNER ADDS

Made for a clear view
of your run

Apple’s workout APIs handle the session and collect the core measurements. Runner turns those inputs into a display you can read at a glance.

Measurements into running metrics

Speed becomes pace. Distance and active time become average pace and lap pace. GPS altitude becomes elevation gain.

Careful handling of GPS data

Runner filters poor-quality positions and implausible jumps before using them for the route and fallback distance. Altitude readings are filtered separately before counting climbs.

A display that respects pauses

Paused time is excluded from pace calculations. Missing or outdated heart rate and current pace readings appear as a dash.
